The project

Making a brain-tumour treatment safer

Gliomas are brain tumours that start in the glial cells. Drugs to treat them exist, but the blood–brain barrier blocks more than 98% of small-molecule drugs from reaching the brain.

MB-FUS (microbubble-enhanced focused ultrasound) gets around this. Tiny gas bubbles are injected, and focused ultrasound makes them vibrate, briefly opening the barrier so the drug can get in.

It is safe under controlled conditions, but in rare cases patients have been seriously harmed, and the exact cause is unknown. Our project finds out why, so doctors can spot at-risk patients before treatment.

  1. 1

    Data collection

    Build a standardised, de-identified dataset of MB-FUS patients.

  2. 2

    Comparative analysis

    Compare patients with and without complications to find the strongest risk factors.

  3. 3

    Risk framework

    Build a model that sorts new patients into low, moderate or high risk.

Your role

What will I do?

  1. Collect

    Pull data from identified sources, and search existing literature for other data we could use.

  2. Clean

    Collate the collected data into the standardised template set by the research team.

  3. Plot

    Do a first round of analysis, plotting graphs and tables to spot notable trends.

  4. Predict

    Use the regression techniques and tools provided to help build the risk-screening framework.
